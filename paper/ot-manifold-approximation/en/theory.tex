\begin{abstract}
How far can a generative decoder be compressed before its latent geometry
breaks?  Reconstruction error does not answer this question: a student can
match a teacher at sampled latent codes while changing the derivatives that
determine interpolation, retrieval, and geodesic distance.  We study
teacher--student diagonal-Gaussian decoders under the 2-Wasserstein metric.
The Gaussian closed form identifies the parameter map
$F=(\mu,\sigma)$ as a Euclidean immersion with pullback metric
$G=J_F^{\trans}J_F$.  A uniform student--teacher Jacobian error then certifies
every intrinsic distance and, through teacher margins, triplet-retrieval
accuracy.  Neural derivative-approximation rates yield the sufficient
geometry--capacity law $P=O(\tau^{-d/(\nu-1)})$ for a $\nu$-smooth teacher on
a $d$-dimensional latent region.

For a fixed smooth decoder trunk, we further prove that rank-constrained joint
output--Jacobian distillation has a closed-form global solution: a truncated
SVD of the covariance-whitened teacher head.  An analytic $28\times28$
Gaussian image manifold gives an exact simultaneous output--distance
threshold over every shared-trunk linear head: at 5\% tolerance, every rank at
most 23 is infeasible, while rank 24 has 4.898\% output error and 4.555\%
distance distortion, reducing head storage by $12.61\times$.  A neural width sweep shows why first-order
supervision matters: on a frequency-warped Gaussian torus, output-only
distillation fails the same 5\% grid test even at $69{,}128$ parameters,
whereas output--Jacobian distillation passes at $18{,}184$.  Finally, on a
trained two-dimensional MNIST VAE, covariance-aware head compression reaches
the sampled 5\% threshold at rank 12 rather than rank 60 for ordinary SVD,
with $4.65\times$ head compression and 99.95\% teacher-triplet agreement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Knowledge distillation, pruning, low-rank factorization, and quantization can
greatly reduce a neural network while retaining its usual test loss.  For a
generative decoder, however, matching individual outputs is only a zeroth-order
requirement.  A latent displacement is measured through the derivative of the
decoder, and the shortest path between two codes depends on those derivatives
along an entire curve.  Two decoders with almost identical reconstruction loss
can therefore support different interpolations and nearest neighbours.

This paper asks one deliberately concrete question:

\begin{quote}
What is the smallest student that preserves every teacher-induced latent
distance to a prescribed relative tolerance $\tau$?
\end{quote}

The phrase ``smallest'' is meaningless without fixing the admissible student
family and numerical precision.  We consequently give two complementary
answers.  First, for a general smooth student family we derive a sufficient
parameter--accuracy rate and state precisely where finite-bit assumptions are
needed.  Second, for the common deployment operation of replacing a decoder's
dense output head by a rank-$r$ factorization, we obtain an exact optimum and a
computable safe rank.  The second result is not an optimizer-dependent neural
architecture search: it follows from one singular value decomposition.

The geometry is defined by optimal transport.  Let a diagonal-Gaussian decoder
be
\begin{equation}
 K_z=\mathcal N\!\left(\mu(z),
 \diag(\sigma(z)^2)\right),
 \qquad z\in\Omega\subset\R^d.
 \label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
For two codes, the exact Gaussian formula gives
\begin{equation}
 W_2(K_z,K_{z'})^2
 =\norm{\mu(z)-\mu(z')}^2
  +\norm{\sigma(z)-\sigma(z')}^2.
 \label{eq:gaussian-w2}
\end{equation}
Thus $F=(\mu,\sigma)$ embeds decoder distributions into Euclidean parameter
space.  The induced latent metric is $G=J_F^{\trans}J_F$, and its path distance
is invariant to a change of latent coordinates.  This is the analytically
tractable version of Wasserstein latent transport.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We define the minimum safe student by simultaneous output and intrinsic
  distance tolerances.  A finite-error theorem turns uniform Jacobian error
  into a multiplicative guarantee for every latent pair.
  \item We introduce a triplet-margin certificate.  It converts a geometric
  distortion bound into a directly reportable accuracy lower bound.  With a
  mild margin condition, neural derivative approximation rates become an
  explicit parameter--accuracy law.
  \item For a fixed decoder trunk and a rank-constrained head, we solve joint
  output--Jacobian distillation globally by a covariance-whitened SVD.  The
  singular-value tail is an exact lower bound for the stated distillation
  problem, and its next singular value yields a safe rank for all distances.
  \item We evaluate the same predeclared 5\% criterion at three levels: an
  exact multi-resolution Gaussian image manifold, trained neural students on
  a warped Gaussian torus, and the frozen decoder head of an MNIST VAE.  This
  separates theorem verification, optimization behavior, and real-data use.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

Classical knowledge distillation transfers soft teacher outputs
\cite{hinton2015}.  Sobolev training and Jacobian matching add derivative
targets \cite{czarnecki2017,srinivas2018}.  Relational and
similarity-preserving distillation match pairwise relations on a finite
transfer set \cite{park2019,tung2019}.  Our use of a Jacobian loss is therefore
not itself new.  The distinction is the guarantee: we connect first-order
distillation to a continuous Wasserstein pullback metric, every intrinsic
path distance, a capacity threshold, and certified retrieval accuracy.

Weighted low-rank factorization already uses task or activation information
instead of applying ordinary SVD directly to a weight matrix
\cite{hsu2022}.  Our covariance-whitened algebra is an instance of this broad
idea.  The new ingredient is to place decoder Jacobians in the covariance and
translate the resulting approximation error into Wasserstein latent-metric
and path-distance guarantees.

Pullback Riemannian metrics for deterministic and stochastic generative models
were developed by Arvanitidis et al.~\cite{arvanitidis2018} and Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2018}.  Optimal Latent Transport computes paths using transport
costs between decoder distributions \cite{roy2022}.  These works analyze or
use the geometry of a trained decoder.  We instead study what happens to that
geometry when the decoder is compressed.  The Gaussian transport identity is
classical \cite{givensshortt1984,takatsu2011}.

Our real-data study uses the variational-autoencoder construction
\cite{kingma2014} and MNIST \cite{lecun1998}.  Its target is explicitly the
geometry of a frozen teacher decoder, not a claim that a two-dimensional VAE
recovers the unknown true manifold of handwritten digits.

Neural approximation in higher-order Sobolev norms provides upper and lower
complexity rates for smooth function classes.  In particular, encodable
weights attain nearly optimal derivative approximation rates
\cite{guhring2021}, and smooth Tanh networks admit explicit high-order bounds
\cite{deryck2021}.  We transfer these function-space rates to latent distance
and task accuracy.  The resulting lower bounds apply to decoder derivative
fidelity; they do not automatically become geometry-only lower bounds because
different immersions can induce the same metric.

\section{Wasserstein Geometry Distillation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $F_T,F_S:\Omega\to\R^N$ denote teacher and student Gaussian parameter
maps, where $N=2n$ in \eqref{eq:decoder}, or $N=n$ when the observation
covariance is fixed.  Assume throughout that $\Omega$ is a compact,
path-connected $C^1$ domain or manifold on which every two points admit a
rectifiable connecting curve, and that $F_T$ is a $C^1$ immersion.  Define
\begin{equation}
 G_T=J_{F_T}^{\trans}J_{F_T},
 \qquad
 G_S=J_{F_S}^{\trans}J_{F_S}.
 \label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
For a piecewise $C^1$ curve $c:[0,1]\to\Omega$, its teacher length and
distance are
\begin{align}
 L_T(c)&=\int_0^1
 \sqrt{\dot c(t)^{\trans}G_T(c(t))\dot c(t)}\dd t,\notag\\
 d_T(z,z')&=\inf_{c(0)=z,\,c(1)=z'}L_T(c),
 \label{eq:path-distance}
\end{align}
with analogous definitions for $S$.

We separate output fidelity from geometry.  Let
\begin{align}
 E_0(S,T)&=\sup_{z\in\Omega}W_2(K_z^S,K_z^T),\notag\\
 D_{\rm pair}(S,T)&=
 \sup_{z\ne z'}
 \left|\frac{d_S(z,z')}{d_T(z,z')}-1\right|.
 \label{eq:two-errors}
\end{align}
Output fidelity is necessary: without $E_0$, an unrelated isometric decoder
would count as successful compression.  For a student class $\mathcal A_P$
with at most $P$ encodable parameters, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 P^\star(\eta,\tau)
 =\min\{P:\;&\exists S\in\mathcal A_P,\\
 &E_0(S,T)\le\eta,\\
 &D_{\rm pair}(S,T)\le\tau\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:min-safe-size}
\end{equation}
The class and bit precision are part of this definition.

For numerical evaluation, no path solver is needed for the stronger local
quantity
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm loc}(S,T)=
 \sup_{z\in\Omega,\,v\ne0}
 \left|
 \sqrt{\frac{v^{\trans}G_S(z)v}{v^{\trans}G_T(z)v}}-1
 \right|.
 \label{eq:local-distortion}
\end{equation}
It is obtained from the extremal generalized eigenvalues of $(G_S,G_T)$.
Integrating lengths shows immediately that
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm loc}(S,T)\le\tau
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D_{\rm pair}(S,T)\le\tau.
 \label{eq:local-implies-global}
\end{equation}

\subsection{A certificate from Jacobian error}

The following result supplies the test used throughout the paper.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform geometry certificate]
\label{thm:stability}
Suppose, uniformly on $\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\min}(J_{F_T})\ge s>0,
 \qquad
 \norm{J_{F_T}}_2\le M_1.
 \label{eq:teacher-conditioning}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_1
 =\sup_{z\in\Omega}
 \norm{J_{F_S}(z)-J_{F_T}(z)}_2
 \label{eq:jacobian-error}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 q=2M_1\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_1^2,
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac{q}{s^2}.
 \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
If $\varepsilon_1<s$ and $\delta<1$, then $F_S$ is an immersion and
\begin{equation}
 (1-\delta)G_T\preceq G_S\preceq(1+\delta)G_T.
 \label{eq:metric-certificate}
\end{equation}
Consequently, every latent pair satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{1-\delta}\,d_T(z,z')
 \le d_S(z,z')
 \le\sqrt{1+\delta}\,d_T(z,z').
 \label{eq:distance-certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $J_S=J_T+E$.  Expansion gives
\[
 \norm{G_S-G_T}_2
 \le2\norm{J_T}_2\norm{E}_2+\norm{E}_2^2
 \le q.
\]
Since $G_T\succeq s^2I$, this yields
\eqref{eq:metric-certificate}.  Taking square roots, integrating along every
curve, and then taking infima proves \eqref{eq:distance-certificate}.
Finally $\sigma_{\min}(J_S)\ge s-\varepsilon_1>0$.
\end{proof}

For a requested relative error $\tau\in(0,1)$, the lower inequality in
\eqref{eq:distance-certificate} is the restrictive one.  Therefore a directly
computable sufficient Jacobian tolerance is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_\tau
 =\sqrt{M_1^2+s^2(2\tau-\tau^2)}-M_1.
 \label{eq:epsilon-tau}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\varepsilon_1\le\varepsilon_\tau$ is equivalent to
$\delta\le2\tau-\tau^2$, and hence $D_{\rm pair}\le\tau$.

\subsection{From geometric distortion to accuracy}

A percentage accuracy makes the consequence of the certificate easier to
interpret.  For a triplet $(q,a,b)$, assume $d_T(q,a)<d_T(q,b)$ and define the
teacher squared-distance margin
\begin{equation}
 \gamma(q,a,b)=
 \frac{d_T(q,b)^2-d_T(q,a)^2}
 {d_T(q,b)^2+d_T(q,a)^2}.
 \label{eq:triplet-margin}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Certified triplet accuracy]
\label{prop:triplet}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}, every triplet satisfying
$\gamma(q,a,b)>\delta$ retains its teacher ordering under the student.  Thus,
for a test set $\mathcal T$, the student triplet agreement is at least
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm cert}(\delta)
 =\frac{1}{\abs{\mathcal T}}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}
 \mathbf 1\{\gamma(t)>\delta\}.
 \label{eq:certified-accuracy}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $A=d_T(q,a)^2$ and $B=d_T(q,b)^2$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:distance-certificate} preserves the ordering whenever
$(1+\delta)A<(1-\delta)B$.  Rearrangement gives
$(B-A)/(B+A)>\delta$, which is exactly \eqref{eq:triplet-margin}.
\end{proof}

Suppose a family of $P$-parameter students simultaneously satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_z\norm{F_S(z)-F_T(z)}
 &\le C_0P^{-\nu/d},\\
 \varepsilon_1(P)&\le C_1P^{-(\nu-1)/d}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:simultaneous-rate}
\end{equation}
for a $\nu$-smooth teacher with $\nu>1$ and an architecture for which these
uniform value and derivative rates hold.  Combining the value bound with
\eqref{eq:epsilon-tau} gives
\begin{equation}
 P^\star(\eta,\tau)
 \lesssim
 \max\left\{
 \left(\frac{C_0}{\eta}\right)^{d/\nu},
 \left(\frac{C_1}{\varepsilon_\tau}\right)^{d/(\nu-1)}
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:joint-capacity-law}
\end{equation}
For fixed output tolerance and small $\tau$, this yields the
geometry-dominated law
\begin{equation}
 P^\star(\eta,\tau)
 =O\!\left(\tau^{-d/(\nu-1)}\right).
 \label{eq:capacity-law}
\end{equation}
The constants, domain assumptions, activation, depth, and logarithmic factors
are inherited from the selected approximation theorem
\cite{guhring2021,deryck2021}; \eqref{eq:joint-capacity-law} is a conditional
transfer of those rates, not a dimension-free architecture claim.

If the empirical or population triplet-margin distribution obeys
$\Pr(\gamma\le t)\le C_\gamma t^\beta$, the derivative rate also implies
\begin{equation}
 1-A_{\rm cert}(P)
 \le C_\gamma\delta(P)^\beta
 =O\!\left(P^{-(\nu-1)\beta/d}\right).
 \label{eq:parameter-accuracy}
\end{equation}
Encodable-weight approximation lower bounds match the derivative exponent up
to logarithms \cite{guhring2021}.  They prove optimality of decoder derivative
approximation, not of geometry alone: rigid motions and other isometries erase
information when $F$ is replaced by $J_F^{\trans}J_F$.

\section{Closed-Form Low-Rank Distillation}
\label{sec:low-rank}

We now fix a setting in which the compression frontier can be computed exactly.
Let a smooth decoder trunk $h:\Omega\to\R^p$ be shared by teacher and student,
and compress only an affine Euclidean parameter head:
\begin{equation}
 F_W(z)=Wh(z)+b,
 \qquad
 F_U(z)=Uh(z)+b,
 \label{eq:linear-heads}
\end{equation}
where $W,U\in\R^{N\times p}$.  A rank-$r$ student can store $U$ as two
factors using $r(N+p)+N$ head parameters including the bias, rather than
$Np+N$.

This setting applies directly to a fixed-covariance Gaussian mean head.  If
$F$ also contains learned standard deviations, positivity of the compressed
channels on $\Omega$ is an additional admissibility condition: unconstrained
SVD does not guarantee it.  A nonlinear softplus variance head is not covered
by the affine closed form.

Given calibration codes $z_1,\ldots,z_m$, write $h_i=h(z_i)$ and
$J_i=J_h(z_i)$.  For $\lambda\ge0$ and $\rho>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 C=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m
 \left(h_ih_i^{\trans}+\lambda J_iJ_i^{\trans}\right)
 +\rho I_p.
 \label{eq:jet-covariance}
\end{equation}
The associated first-order distillation loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_C(U)=\norm{(U-W)C^{1/2}}_F^2.
 \label{eq:distillation-loss}
\end{equation}
Expanding \eqref{eq:jet-covariance} shows that this is the sum of teacher
output error, $\lambda$ times Jacobian error, and a ridge term.

\begin{theorem}[Exact rank--distillation frontier]
\label{thm:rank-distillation}
Let $B=WC^{1/2}$ and let $B_r$ be its rank-$r$ truncated SVD.  Then
\begin{equation}
 U_r=B_rC^{-1/2}
 \label{eq:optimal-head}
\end{equation}
is a global minimizer of \eqref{eq:distillation-loss} over all heads of rank at
most $r$, and
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\rank(U)\le r}\mathcal L_C(U)
 =\sum_{j>r}\sigma_j(B)^2.
 \label{eq:exact-tail}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $V=UC^{1/2}$.  Since $C$ is positive definite,
$\rank(V)=\rank(U)$ and $\mathcal L_C(U)=\norm{V-B}_F^2$.  The result is the
Eckart--Young--Mirsky theorem \cite{eckart1936,mirsky1960}, followed by the
inverse change of variables.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:exact-tail} is a genuine impossibility result within the
fixed-trunk, rank-$r$, first-order distillation problem.  It is not a lower
bound over arbitrary decoders that merely share the same intrinsic metric.

The same SVD also produces a distance certificate.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \ell_1=\sup_{z\in\Omega}
 \norm{C^{-1/2}J_h(z)}_2,
 \qquad
 \overline\varepsilon_r=\sigma_{r+1}(B)\ell_1.
 \label{eq:spectral-jacobian-bound}
\end{equation}
Because $U_r-W=(B_r-B)C^{-1/2}$ and
$\norm{B_r-B}_2=\sigma_{r+1}(B)$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_z\norm{J_{F_{U_r}}(z)-J_{F_W}(z)}_2
 \le\overline\varepsilon_r.
 \label{eq:spectral-to-uniform}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Certified safe rank]
\label{cor:safe-rank}
Let the teacher map $F_W$ satisfy \eqref{eq:teacher-conditioning}.  If
$\overline\varepsilon_r\le\varepsilon_\tau$, then the rank-$r$ head
\eqref{eq:optimal-head} preserves every teacher intrinsic distance within
relative error $\tau$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 r_{\rm cert}(\tau)
 =\min\left\{r:
 \sigma_{r+1}(B)\ell_1\le\varepsilon_\tau
 \right\}
 \label{eq:certified-rank}
\end{equation}
is a safe rank along the SVD-optimal distillation frontier, provided certified
global bounds on $s$, $M_1$, and $\ell_1$ are available.  Replacing the
suprema by sample maxima yields an empirical test, not a continuum
certificate.
\end{corollary}

An analogous value bound follows by replacing $J_h$ with $h$.  Quantization
can also be included by adding the uniform Jacobian perturbation of the
quantized factors to $\overline\varepsilon_r$; minimizing total storage then
chooses rank and bits under the same inequality.

\section{An Exact Gaussian Image Task}
\label{sec:exact-task}

To test a capacity theorem, a real dataset alone is insufficient because its
true decoder geometry is unknown.  We therefore construct a high-dimensional
image decoder for which output error, rank lower bounds, and every distance
are analytic.  Let $z=(x,y)$ lie on the flat two-torus
$(\R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$ and define the $4T$ trunk features
\begin{equation}
 h_T(x,y)=
 \bigl(\cos(jx),\sin(jx),\cos(jy),\sin(jy)\bigr)_{j=1}^T.
 \label{eq:fourier-trunk}
\end{equation}
Let $Q\in\R^{784\times4T}$ have orthonormal image atoms as columns and let
$a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_T>0$.  The teacher's $28\times28$ mean image is
\begin{equation}
 \mu_T(z)=\mu_0+QD_T h_T(z),
 \label{eq:image-teacher}
\end{equation}
where $D_T$ repeats $a_j/j$ four times for each $j$.  We use a fixed positive
observation variance, so
$K_z^T=\mathcal N(\mu_T(z),\sigma_0^2I)$ and the exact $W_2$ cost is Euclidean
mean-image distance.  The rank-$4K$ prefix student retains the first $K$
frequency blocks with their teacher coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Exact output--distance law]
\label{prop:exact-rank-law}
Set
\begin{equation}
 S_K=\sum_{j=1}^K a_j^2.
 \label{eq:spectral-energy}
\end{equation}
Then the teacher and student metrics are
\begin{equation}
 G_T=S_TI_2,
 \qquad
 G_K=S_KI_2,
 \label{eq:torus-metrics}
\end{equation}
and for every distinct latent pair,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d_K(z,z')}{d_T(z,z')}
 =\sqrt{\frac{S_K}{S_T}}.
 \label{eq:exact-distance-ratio}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm loc}=D_{\rm pair}
 =1-\sqrt{\frac{S_K}{S_T}},
 \label{eq:exact-distortion}
\end{equation}
and the uniform teacher-output error is
\begin{equation}
 E_{0,K}
 =\left(2\sum_{j>K}\left(\frac{a_j}{j}\right)^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:exact-output-error}
\end{equation}
Thus the first prefix satisfying both tolerances is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 r_{\rm prefix}^\star(\eta,\tau)
 =4\min\{K:\;&E_{0,K}\le\eta,\\
                 &S_K\ge(1-\tau)^2S_T\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exact-min-rank}
\end{equation}
Moreover, under the uniform latent distribution, every shared-trunk rank-$r$
head satisfies
\begin{equation}
 E_0\ge
 \left(\frac12\sum_{\ell>r}\sigma_\ell(W)^2\right)^{1/2},
 \label{eq:output-rank-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
where $W=QD_T$ has singular values $a_j/j$, each repeated four times.  For
pure Jacobian distillation, define separately
$C_J=\E[J_hJ_h^{\trans}]$.  The singular values of $WC_J^{1/2}$ are
$a_j/\sqrt2$, each repeated four times, and the exact optimal tail loss at
rank $4K$ is $2\sum_{j>K}a_j^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each $x$ block,
\[
 \left\|\partial_x
 \left[\frac{a_j}{j}(\cos(jx),\sin(jx))\right]\right\|^2
 =a_j^2.
\]
The $x$ and $y$ image atoms are orthogonal, so cross terms vanish.  Summing
over blocks gives \eqref{eq:torus-metrics}.  All curve lengths are therefore
scaled by $\sqrt{S_K/S_T}$, proving \eqref{eq:exact-distance-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:exact-distortion}.  Orthonormality of $Q$ and
$\cos^2+\sin^2=1$ give \eqref{eq:exact-output-error}.  Under normalized
uniform measure, $\E[h_Th_T^{\trans}]=I/2$, so Eckart--Young--Mirsky gives the
minimum root-mean-square output error in
\eqref{eq:output-rank-lower-bound}; $E_0$ is at least this RMS value.  The
pure-Jacobian statement follows by integrating the squared derivatives.
\end{proof}

The benchmark task is intentionally binary and auditable.  For a tolerance
$\tau$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{PairAcc}@\tau
 =\frac1{\abs{\mathcal P}}
 \sum_{(z,z')\in\mathcal P}
 \mathbf1\left\{
 \left|\frac{d_K(z,z')}{d_T(z,z')}-1\right|\le\tau
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:pair-accuracy}
\end{equation}
Because \eqref{eq:exact-distance-ratio} holds for every pair, this accuracy is
zero below the prefix distance threshold and one at or above it.  Together
with \eqref{eq:output-rank-lower-bound}, the numerical choice in
Section~\ref{sec:controlled-experiment} will establish a true minimum rank over
all shared-trunk linear heads for simultaneous output and distance fidelity.
This is not a claim that all real decoder manifolds have uniform scale.
