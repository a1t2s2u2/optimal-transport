\begin{abstract}
A two-dimensional latent plot is drawn in the coordinates chosen by an
encoder, not in the geometry seen by its decoder.  We study the smallest
setting in which that distortion can be re-expressed and displayed: a known
triangulation of a two-dimensional latent region, local 2-Wasserstein
distances between decoded measures, and a triangular surface in
$\mathbb R^3$.  The output is not a hidden true shape.  It is
a three-dimensional representative of the intrinsic piecewise flat metric defined by the
decoder's Wasserstein edge lengths.

Our theoretical link has two parts.  For diagonal-Gaussian decoders, the
distance built from edge $W_2$ values converges to the continuous pullback
distance at relative rate $O(h)$ on uniformly shape-regular mesh families.
For any returned realization, standard facewise affine singular values then
bound every intrinsic
distance, while incident angle changes bound angle-defect curvature-mass
change.  The latter audit is computed from the input and output and needs no
small-error assumption.  A sufficiently small validated numerical OT error
$\varepsilon$ extends it to ideal edge-$W_2$ values.  The construction and
finite a posteriori audit are optimizer-independent and require neither
decoder derivatives, constant curvature, convexity, nor a known
three-dimensional target.  The Gaussian consistency rate separately assumes
derivative bounds.

We evaluate the construction first on a diagonal-Gaussian decoder whose
Wasserstein geometry is an exactly known surface with spatially varying
positive and negative curvature.  We then train a two-dimensional VAE on
MNIST and visualize the digit-3 region using unregularized discrete OT between
decoded images viewed as masses on the pixel plane.  The figures show the
flat input coordinates, decoded images, the metric-fitted surface, curvature,
and the optimization loss in one auditable pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A latent coordinate is a map coordinate.  It need not measure the change in
the object produced by the decoder.  Two equal steps in a VAE plot can move
very different amounts of image mass, just as equal displacements on a flat
world map can represent different travel distances.  Pullback geometry makes
this precise by differentiating the decoder and defining a Riemannian metric
from its Jacobian \cite{arvanitidis2018,chen2018,roy2022}.  The resulting
metric tensors, magnification factors, and geodesics are informative, but a
metric tensor is not yet a visible surface.

We ask one deliberately narrow question:
\begin{quote}
Given a two-dimensional latent mesh and local Wasserstein distances between
its decoded outputs, how faithfully can those distances and their variable
curvature be displayed by a surface in $\mathbb R^3$?
\end{quote}
The word \emph{displayed} is essential.  An intrinsic metric generally does
not select a unique embedding in three dimensions: a sheet can bend without
changing any intrinsic length.  Conversely, an arbitrary decoder-induced
metric need not admit an exact three-dimensional realization at all.  We
therefore define correctness by quantities that are observable before a
ground-truth shape is known: local OT-length residual, intrinsic-distance
distortion, and angle-defect curvature distortion.

The construction continues the following finite-to-infinitesimal chain:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 z&\longmapsto K_z
 \longmapsto W_2(K_z,K_{z'})
 \longmapsto \text{piecewise flat metric}\\
 &\longmapsto \text{curvature}
 \longmapsto \mathbb R^3\text{ display}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:paper-chain}
\end{equation}
Here $K_z$ is a probability measure constructed by the decoder, and
\emph{piecewise flat} means that every triangle carries the Euclidean metric
fixed by its three edge lengths, with the triangles glued along shared edges.
The first two arrows are the Wasserstein latent geometry developed for
probabilistic decoders.  Our contribution is to couple these standard
piecewise flat constructions to decoder-induced Wasserstein observations and a
continuous Gaussian consistency limit.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We define a \emph{Wasserstein latent surface} directly from local
  decoded-measure distances on a two-dimensional triangulation.  It requires
  decoder evaluations and OT solves, but no decoder derivatives.
  \item For smooth diagonal-Gaussian decoders, we prove $O(h)$ consistency of
  the finite distance built from edge $W_2$ values with the continuous
  Wasserstein pullback distance on uniformly shape-regular mesh families.
  \item We adapt the standard facewise distortion audit for piecewise affine
  maps to this setting.  Singular values bound all intrinsic distances, incident-angle
  changes bound curvature mass, and a supplied OT error $\varepsilon$ extends
  the audit to ideal edge-$W_2$ values.
  \item We give two visible experiments: an exact mixed-curvature Gaussian
  benchmark and an MNIST digit-3 surface based on spatial transport of decoded
  ink.  The latter is intentionally one digit rather than the unsupported
  assumption that all ten classes form one smooth manifold.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Geometry of generative models.}
Latent Space Oddity and subsequent work pull an observation-space metric back
through a deterministic or stochastic decoder, then compute geodesics and
volume distortion \cite{arvanitidis2018,chen2018,arvanitidis2022}.
Optimal Latent Transport instead pulls back Wasserstein geometry from decoder
distributions \cite{roy2022}.  Identifiability results show that metric
structure can remain meaningful when latent coordinates are not
\cite{syrota2025}.  These works motivate the metric in
Equation~\eqref{eq:paper-chain}.  Our target is different: a finite 3D
visualization and an a posteriori curvature audit, without evaluating a
Jacobian.

\paragraph{Learning from distances.}
Isomap reconstructs low-dimensional coordinates from graph distances
\cite{tenenbaum2000}.  Most directly, Wassmap replaces Euclidean dissimilarity
by pairwise Wasserstein distance to obtain approximately isometric
image-manifold coordinates \cite{hamm2023wassmap}, and LOT Wassmap accelerates
that construction with linearized transport under an approximation guarantee
\cite{cloninger2025}.  Recovering a manifold, or its metric, from noisy
intrinsic distances also has finite-sample and asymptotic guarantees
\cite{qiu2023,fefferman2020,hamm2025}.  All of these infer coordinates or a
manifold from distance data.  We instead keep a prescribed two-dimensional
mesh, specialize the observations to decoded measures, and audit the
distortion of the returned variable-curvature piecewise flat surface; we do not claim to
recover unknown sample parameters or topology.

\paragraph{Piecewise-affine mesh distortion.}
Facewise Jacobian singular values are standard tools for measuring and
constraining distortion in triangular mesh parameterization
\cite{lipman2012}.  We connect this established language to decoder-induced
Wasserstein edge geometry, compose it with the continuous Gaussian pullback
limit, and report the resulting audit together with angle-defect curvature.

\paragraph{Discrete OT and curvature.}
Discrete Kantorovich transport gives a natural distance between images viewed
as masses on a pixel grid \cite{peyre2019}.  We use an unregularized
linear-program solver through POT \cite{flamary2021}, so entropic bias is absent from the
reported MNIST distances.  Angle defect is the intrinsic curvature mass of a
piecewise flat surface.  Its convergence to smooth Gaussian curvature requires mesh
conditions and is not pointwise reliable on an arbitrary triangulation
\cite{gawlik2020,hartig2021}.  Accordingly, our audit first treats the
finite angle-defect curvature mass.  Area normalization is presented only with its
stronger noise scaling made explicit.

\section{Wasserstein Geometry on a Latent Mesh}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $\cZ\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a latent region and
\begin{equation}
 K:\cZ\longrightarrow\cP_2(\mathcal Y),\qquad z\longmapsto K_z,
 \label{eq:decoder-measure-map}
\end{equation}
where $(\mathcal Y,d_{\mathcal Y})$ is a complete separable ground space.
The map may be the conditional distribution of a probabilistic decoder, or a
probability measure that we define explicitly from a decoded object.  The latter
is used for MNIST: normalized grayscale intensities are masses at pixel
locations.  These two interpretations are not conflated.

The decoder induces the pseudometric
\begin{equation}
 d_K(z,z'):=W_2(K_z,K_{z'}).
 \label{eq:decoder-pseudometric}
\end{equation}
It is a metric if $K$ is injective.  Injectivity is not needed below; a zero
edge simply has to be excluded from a nondegenerate triangle.

\subsection{Connection to the continuous pullback metric}

For a diagonal-Gaussian decoder with componentwise positive $\sigma(z)$,
\begin{equation}
 K_z=\mathcal N\!\left(m(z),\diag(\sigma(z)^2)\right),
 \qquad F(z)=(m(z),\sigma(z)),
 \label{eq:diagonal-gaussian-decoder}
\end{equation}
the Gaussian transport formula gives \cite{givensshortt1984,takatsu2011}
\begin{equation}
 W_2(K_z,K_{z'})=\norm{F(z)-F(z')}.
 \label{eq:gaussian-chord}
\end{equation}
If $F$ is a smooth immersion, its pullback metric is
$G(z)=J_F(z)^{\trans}J_F(z)$ and
\begin{equation}
 W_2(K_z,K_{z+h})^2
 =h^{\trans}G(z)h+o(\norm h^2).
 \label{eq:local-w2-expansion}
\end{equation}
Thus local $W_2$ queries are finite observations of the same metric that a
Jacobian calculation would produce.  The a posteriori audit later does not
require Gaussianity or differentiability; Equation~\eqref{eq:local-w2-expansion}
only motivates why sufficiently local $W_2$ values are relevant.

The Gaussian case also admits a precise continuous-to-finite statement.  Let
$\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a compact connected polygonal region and assume
that the map $F$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:diagonal-gaussian-decoder} extends to a
$C^2$ map from a neighbourhood of $\Omega$ into $\mathbb R^{2n}$.  Denote its
pullback distance by $d_G$.  Consider a conforming family
$\{\mathcal T_h\}_{h\downarrow0}$ of triangulations of $\Omega$ with uniformly
bounded shape-regularity constant and maximum parameter-space diameter $h$.
Let $F_h$ be the nodal piecewise affine interpolant of $F$ on $\mathcal T_h$.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian mesh consistency]
\label{prop:gaussian-mesh-consistency}
Assume
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{z\in\Omega}\sigma_{\min}(J_F(z))\ge m>0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z\in\Omega}\norm{D^2F(z)}\le H.
 \label{eq:gaussian-consistency-assumptions}
\end{equation}
For each mesh edge $(i,j)$, set $\ell_{h,ij}$ equal to
$W_2(K_{z_i},K_{z_j})$.  There is a constant $C_I$, depending only on the
uniform shape-regularity bound, such that, if
$\eta_h:=C_IHh/m<1$, these lengths form nondegenerate triangles.  Let
$M_{\ell_h}$ be their abstract glued complex and let
$\iota_h:\Omega\to M_{\ell_h}$ be the piecewise affine homeomorphism that preserves vertex
labels and barycentric coordinates facewise.  Then
\begin{equation}
 (1-\eta_h)d_G(p,q)
 \le d_{\ell_h}(\iota_hp,\iota_hq)
 \le(1+\eta_h)d_G(p,q)
 \label{eq:gaussian-mesh-consistency}
\end{equation}
for all $p,q\in\Omega$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expansion after mapping to a shape-regular reference triangle gives the
standard nodal interpolation estimate \cite{ciarlet1978}
$\norm{D F_h-D F}\le C_IHh$ almost everywhere on each face.  Hence, for every
tangent vector $v$,
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\norm{D F_hv}-\norm{D Fv}}
 \le C_IHh\norm v
 \le\eta_h\norm{D Fv}.
\end{equation}
The reverse triangle inequality also gives
$\norm{D F_hv}\ge(m-C_IHh)\norm v>0$, so every affine face image is
nondegenerate.  Equation~\eqref{eq:gaussian-chord} shows that its edge lengths
are exactly $\ell_h$.  Consequently, each abstract face is isometric to the
corresponding affine image under $F_h$, and the length of
$\iota_h\circ\gamma$ is
$\int\norm{D F_h(\gamma(t))\dot\gamma(t)}\,dt$.  Integrating the displayed
pointwise inequalities along every rectifiable parameter-space path and taking
infima gives Equation~\eqref{eq:gaussian-mesh-consistency}.
\end{proof}

Neither $F$ nor $F_h$ needs to be globally injective: the compared object is
the abstract glued metric, not the ambient image with intersection
shortcuts.

Consequently, suppose a displayed assignment $\widetilde\ell$ on the same
abstract mesh also has nondegenerate faces.  Compute $s_-,s_+$ as defined
below, with $\ell_h$ in place of $\widehat\ell$, let
$\Psi_h:M_{\ell_h}\to M_{\widetilde\ell}$ be the barycentric piecewise affine homeomorphism,
and set
$\Phi_h=\Psi_h\circ\iota_h$.  Then, for all $p,q\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 s_-(1-\eta_h)d_G(p,q)
 &\le d_{\widetilde\ell}(\Phi_hp,\Phi_hq)\\
 &\le s_+(1+\eta_h)d_G(p,q).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:end-to-end-gaussian-display}
\end{equation}
Thus a sequence of displayed metrics converges to $d_G$ only when its measured
facewise factors also approach one; no optimizer guarantee is asserted here.
Unlike $(s_-,s_+,\Delta_i)$, the factor $\eta_h$ is a priori: it needs the
derivative bounds $m$ and $H$, and it exploits the Euclidean representation
specific to diagonal Gaussians, so it does not by itself cover the
discrete-image MNIST map or a general Wasserstein-valued decoder.  No
pointwise-curvature conclusion follows from this distance result alone.

\subsection{Finite input and angle-defect curvature}

Let $\mathcal T=(V,E,F)$ be a finite, connected triangulation of a compact
two-dimensional manifold, possibly with boundary, and associate $z_i\in\cZ$
and $K_i:=K_{z_i}$ with every vertex.  The unknown exact edge length and its
numerical observation are
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{ij}^{*}=W_2(K_i,K_j),
 \qquad \widehat\ell_{ij}\simeq\ell_{ij}^{*}
 \quad ((i,j)\in E).
 \label{eq:true-observed-lengths}
\end{equation}
Any positive edge-length assignment $\ell$ whose three lengths on every face
satisfy strict triangle inequalities defines an intrinsic piecewise flat metric
$M_\ell=(\mathcal T,d_\ell)$ by gluing the corresponding Euclidean triangles,
without choosing coordinates in an ambient space.  We assume that $\ell^*$ is
such an assignment; the validity of observed and displayed assignments is
audited below.

This construction uses direct Wasserstein distances only on mesh edges.  At
nonadjacent vertices, $d_{\ell^*}(i,j)$ is in general neither the direct value
$W_2(K_i,K_j)$ nor a continuous pullback geodesic distance;
Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-mesh-consistency} closes that gap only for
smooth diagonal-Gaussian immersions.  The finite audit below concerns
precisely the edge-$W_2$-induced piecewise flat metric just defined.

For $f=(i,j,k)$, the angle at $i$ is fixed by the cosine law,
\begin{equation}
 \theta_i^{jk}(\ell)
 =\arccos\!\left(
 \frac{\ell_{ij}^2+\ell_{ik}^2-\ell_{jk}^2}
 {2\ell_{ij}\ell_{ik}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:finite-angle}
\end{equation}
At an interior vertex, the curvature mass is
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i(\ell)=2\pi-\sum_{f\ni i}\theta_{i,f}(\ell).
 \label{eq:finite-curvature}
\end{equation}
For a boundary vertex, $2\pi$ is replaced by $\pi$; the result is boundary
geodesic-curvature mass, not Gaussian curvature.  We report interior vertices
in the main experiment.  With barycentric vertex area
$A_i=\frac13\sum_{f\ni i}A_f$, one may form the curvature density
$\mathcal K_i=\kappa_i/A_i$, but $\kappa_i$ is the primary finite quantity.

\section{An Auditable 3D Display}
\label{sec:certificate}

Let $Q\supseteq E$ be the set of local pairs queried for visualization, with
$\widehat\ell_{ij}>0$ for every $(i,j)\in Q$.  We construct coordinates
$X=(x_i)_{i\in V}$, $x_i\in\mathbb R^3$, by minimizing the dimensionless local
stress
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_Q(X)
 =\frac1{|Q|}\sum_{(i,j)\in Q}
 \left(
  \frac{\norm{x_i-x_j}-\widehat\ell_{ij}}
  {\widehat\ell_{ij}}
 \right)^2
 +\lambda\mathcal R_{\rm display}(X).
 \label{eq:display-objective}
\end{equation}
The optional $\mathcal R_{\rm display}$ selects a visually smooth solution; it
does not enter the theorem.  A separate curvature loss is unnecessary.  If
the three edge lengths of every face agree, all face angles and angle defects
already agree.

Denote the displayed edge length by
$\widetilde\ell_{ij}=\norm{x_i-x_j}$ and define
\begin{align}
 \varepsilon
 &=\max_{e\in E}\abs{\widehat\ell_e-\ell_e^*},
 \label{eq:ot-error}\\
 \delta
 &=\max_{e\in E}\abs{\widetilde\ell_e-\widehat\ell_e}.
 \label{eq:display-error}
\end{align}
The first term contains numerical OT approximation error for the fixed
measures $K_i$; the second is observable after visualization.  By the triangle
inequality, $\max_{e\in E}\abs{\widetilde\ell_e-\ell_e^*}\le\varepsilon+\delta$.

The scalar $\delta$ is useful but unnecessarily pessimistic.  A sharper
certificate is still finite and directly computable.  For a labelled face
$f=(i,j,k)$ and any valid length assignment $\ell$, put
\begin{equation}
 P_f(\ell)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \ell_{ij} & a_f(\ell)\\
  0 & b_f(\ell)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \quad
 \begin{aligned}
 a_f(\ell)&=\frac{\ell_{ij}^2+\ell_{ik}^2-\ell_{jk}^2}
 {2\ell_{ij}},\\
 b_f(\ell)&=\sqrt{\ell_{ik}^2-a_f(\ell)^2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:canonical-triangle}
\end{equation}
Its columns place the edges from $i$ in a canonical Euclidean triangle.
Define the facewise affine distortion and its global extrema by
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 T_f&=P_f(\widetilde\ell)P_f(\widehat\ell)^{-1},\\
 s_-&:=\min_{f\in F}\sigma_{\min}(T_f),\\
 s_+&:=\max_{f\in F}\sigma_{\max}(T_f).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:face-distortion}
\end{equation}
Changing the planar placement of either labelled triangle only multiplies
$T_f$ by orthogonal matrices and does not change its singular values.  At a
vertex, define the angle-change bound
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_i:=\sum_{f\ni i}
 \abs{\theta_{i,f}(\widetilde\ell)-
       \theta_{i,f}(\widehat\ell)}.
 \label{eq:posterior-only-certificate}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[A posteriori distortion of a fixed realization]
\label{prop:pl-certificate}
Assume that both $\widehat\ell$ and $\widetilde\ell$ satisfy strict triangle
inequalities on every face.  Let
$\Phi:M_{\widehat\ell}\to M_{\widetilde\ell}$ be the piecewise affine homeomorphism that
preserves vertex labels and barycentric coordinates within corresponding
faces.  Then $0<s_-\le s_+<\infty$ and, for every $p,q$ in the abstract glued
complex,
\begin{equation}
 s_-d_{\widehat\ell}(p,q)
 \le d_{\widetilde\ell}(\Phi p,\Phi q)
 \le s_+d_{\widehat\ell}(p,q).
 \label{eq:distance-certificate}
\end{equation}
At every interior vertex,
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\kappa_i(\widetilde\ell)-\kappa_i(\widehat\ell)}
 \le \Delta_i.
 \label{eq:curvature-certificate}
\end{equation}
The curvature inequality also holds at a boundary vertex when both sides are
interpreted as boundary geodesic-curvature mass.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Nondegeneracy makes every $T_f$ invertible.  On each face, its singular values
bound the length change of every tangent vector.  Apply the bound face by face
to any rectifiable path, then take the infimum over paths.  Since $\Phi$ is a
homeomorphism, applying the same argument to all inverse-image paths gives
both sides of Equation~\eqref{eq:distance-certificate}.  In the curvature
difference the constants $2\pi$, or $\pi$ at the boundary, cancel.  The
triangle inequality over incident angle changes gives
Equation~\eqref{eq:curvature-certificate}.
\end{proof}

This proposition has no small-error or shape-regularity premise: a nearly
degenerate face is exposed by a small $s_-$ or a large $s_+$ and $\Delta_i$.
The distances are those of the abstract glued complexes.  If displayed triangles
self-intersect in $\mathbb R^3$, an intersection does not create a shortcut
between unrelated abstract faces.

To connect the computed lengths to ideal Wasserstein values, suppose the true
and observed triangles have edges in $[\ell_{\min},\ell_{\max}]$, angles at
least $\alpha_0>0$, and
$\eta_\varepsilon:=\varepsilon/\ell_{\min}\le
\eta_0(\ell_{\max}/\ell_{\min},\alpha_0)$.  Smooth dependence of triangle
angles and Gram matrices on side lengths gives constants $C_\theta,C_g$,
depending only on these uniform bounds.  If $d_i$ is the number of faces
incident to $i$, then
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\kappa_i(\widetilde\ell)-\kappa_i(\ell^*)}
 \le \Delta_i+C_\theta d_i\eta_\varepsilon
 \label{eq:ideal-curvature-certificate}
\end{equation}
and, for the corresponding barycentric map
$\Phi^*:M_{\ell^*}\to M_{\widetilde\ell}$ and all
$p,q\in M_{\ell^*}$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 s_-(1-C_g\eta_\varepsilon)d_{\ell^*}(p,q)
 &\le d_{\widetilde\ell}(\Phi^*p,\Phi^*q)\\
 &\le s_+(1+C_g\eta_\varepsilon)d_{\ell^*}(p,q).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ideal-distance-certificate}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta_0$ is chosen so that the lower factor is positive.  Thus the exact
a posteriori quantities $(s_-,s_+,\Delta_i)$ certify the computed OT geometry;
a validated OT error bound $\varepsilon$ extends them to the ideal edge-$W_2$
geometry.  Floating-point evaluations without outward rounding are reported
as computed diagnostics of these theoretical bounds, not as formal interval
certificates.

On a quasi-uniform mesh of scale $h$ with bounded degree, and if the true,
observed, and displayed triangles remain in one uniformly shape-regular class
with $r:=\varepsilon+\delta$ small, the same perturbation argument gives
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\widetilde\kappa_i-\kappa_i^*}=O\!\left(\frac{r}{h}\right),
 \qquad
 \abs{\widetilde{\mathcal K}_i-\mathcal K_i^*}=O\!\left(\frac{r}{h^3}\right),
 \label{eq:density-scaling}
\end{equation}
the second from $A_i^*,\widetilde A_i\asymp h^2$,
$\abs{\widetilde A_i-A_i^*}=O(hr)$, and $\abs{\kappa_i^*}=O(1)$.  Thus, under
adversarial fixed-relative edge noise, refinement keeps distance distortion at
the same order while an unsmoothed pointwise curvature heat map can become
worse.  Our primary finite quantity is curvature mass.

\subsection{What the display does not identify}

Even $\varepsilon=\delta=0$ identifies only intrinsic geometry: two triangles
sharing an edge can rotate about it while keeping every face length, angle,
intrinsic distance, and curvature mass fixed.  A smoothness or height-field
constraint therefore selects a representative; it is not new geometric
evidence.

Whether the \emph{complete} matrix $D_{ij}=W_2(K_i,K_j)$ is realized exactly in
$\mathbb R^3$ is in turn decided by classical multidimensional scaling: the
double-centered Gram matrix $-\frac12H(D\circ D)H$, with
$H=I-|V|^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\trans}$, must be positive semidefinite of rank
at most three \cite{liberti2014}.  A general Wasserstein metric need not pass
this test, which is unavailable from local edges alone: a zero edge residual
does not make the unobserved complete matrix Euclidean.  The Gaussian
benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:controlled-experiment} passes it by
construction; we do not apply it to MNIST, where
we claim neither a unique external shape nor a true data-manifold curvature.
