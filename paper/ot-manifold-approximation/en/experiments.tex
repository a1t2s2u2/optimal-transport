\section{Experiments: From a Flat Input to a Visible Surface}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments answer two separate questions.  The controlled problem asks
whether the full pipeline recovers a known variable-curvature surface when
its assumptions are exactly true.  MNIST asks what the same definition shows
for a learned neural decoder when no external shape is known.  Ground-truth
3D coordinates are used only in the controlled evaluation.

\subsection{Visualization and evaluation protocol}

For both data sets, the input is
\begin{equation}
 (\{z_i\}_{i\in V},\mathcal T,
   \{W_2(K_i,K_j):(i,j)\in Q\}),
 \label{eq:experiment-input}
\end{equation}
and the output is a coordinate $x_i\in\mathbb R^3$ for every input vertex,
with the same triangular faces.  The theoretical edges $E$ are always in
$Q$.  We fit each representation's free parameters by deterministic Adam
followed by full-batch L-BFGS, use three fixed initializations, and select the
smallest stated
objective.  Backpropagation is merely the numerical solver used here; neither
the problem definition nor Proposition~\ref{prop:pl-certificate} assumes it.

We compare up to four representations.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Scaled latent 2D} whitens the supplied latent coordinates and
  chooses one positive global scale to minimize the relative stress.  This
  invertible affine normalization leaves the flat map and its topology intact;
  it does not fit vertices independently.
  \item \emph{Free 2D} fits every planar vertex independently.  It is an
  unconstrained low-stress reference for flat coordinates, not a valid map when faces collapse or
  reverse orientation; we report those failures explicitly on MNIST.
  \item \emph{Smooth 3D} adds a weak bending term and a face-collapse barrier
  to the local stress.  With
  consistently oriented adjacent faces and unit normals $n_f,n_g$, the term is
  \begin{equation}
   \mathcal B(X)=\frac1{|A|}\sum_{(f,g)\in A}(1-n_f^{\trans}n_g),
   \label{eq:bending-convention}
  \end{equation}
  where $A$ is the set of adjacent face pairs.  This term selects a readable
  branch; it is not evidence about the intrinsic metric.
  \item \emph{Free 3D} minimizes stress without bending.  It is an
  unconstrained low-stress reference, but it may fold like origami and is
  not used as the main visualization.
\end{enumerate}
For the controlled graph surface, the known height-field branch is also a
natural display convention.  MNIST uses no target shape to select bending.
The smooth 3D fit adds a squared hinge when
$\sigma_{\min}(T_f)<0.45$.
Over a fixed coefficient grid, we retain candidates whose fifth percentile of
adjacent-normal inner products is at least $0.25$ and whose global $s_-$ is at
least $0.35$, then select the one with lowest edge stress.
Figure~\ref{fig:visualization-diagnostics} shows the full sweep.

The primary fidelity statistic is
\begin{equation}
 S_Q(X)=\left[
 \frac1{|Q|}\sum_{(i,j)\in Q}
 \left(
 \frac{\norm{x_i-x_j}-\widehat\ell_{ij}}
 {\widehat\ell_{ij}}
 \right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:reported-stress}
\end{equation}
The certificate reports the extreme facewise affine singular values
$(s_-,s_+)$ and the no-cancellation curvature bound $\Delta_i$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:pl-certificate}.  We also report maximum relative
triangle-edge error, interior curvature-mass RMSE, and all-pairs relative
error of shortest paths on the edge graph.  The last statistic is an easily
reproducible graph diagnostic; the theorem itself applies to the full
facewise PL distance.  In the controlled problem we add rigidly aligned
coordinate RMSE.  No coordinate RMSE is defined for MNIST.

\subsection{Controlled mixed-curvature Gaussian decoder}
\label{sec:controlled-experiment}

Take a $21\times21$ grid on $[-1,1]^2$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h(u,v)={}&0.48e^{-6.4((u+0.42)^2+1.15(v-0.24)^2)}\\
 &+0.38e^{-7.2(1.2(u-0.38)^2+(v+0.30)^2)}
 +0.30uv.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:controlled-height}
\end{equation}
The two local peaks and saddle term produce positive and negative curvature
in different regions.  We then declare the diagonal-Gaussian decoder
\begin{equation}
 K_{u,v}=\mathcal N\!\left(
 (u,v),\diag((0.8+h(u,v))^2,0.8^2)
 \right).
 \label{eq:controlled-decoder}
\end{equation}
The first standard deviation remains positive on the entire grid.  By
Equation~\eqref{eq:gaussian-chord},
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 W_2(K_{u,v},K_{u',v'})^2
 ={}&(u-u')^2+(v-v')^2\\
 &+(h(u,v)-h(u',v'))^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:controlled-w2-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus the exact Wasserstein geometry is the chord geometry of the graph
$(u,v,h(u,v))$, while the decoder mean alone remains flat.  The estimator is
given local pairs within four grid steps, $14{,}060$ of the $97{,}020$ total
pairs (14.49\%).  The hidden height is withheld until alignment.

Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-visualization} makes the input and output explicit.
The same OT-derived curvature color is drawn first on the flat input, then on
the hidden target and reconstructed output.  The aligned overlay is an
evaluation panel, not an estimator input.

\subsection{MNIST: unregularized transport of decoded ink}
\label{sec:mnist-experiment}

We train the smooth two-dimensional VAE of
\cite{kingma2014} on 30,000 MNIST training images \cite{lecun1998} for 12
epochs.  Its fully connected encoder has widths $784$--$128$--$64$ followed
by two-dimensional mean and log-variance heads; its decoder has widths
$2$--$64$--$64$--$784$, with $\tanh$ hidden activations.  We optimize mean
squared reconstruction error plus $3\times10^{-3}$ times the KL term using
AdamW with learning rate $2\times10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-6}$, and batch
size 256.  The encoder posterior means of test-set digit 3 define a centered
elliptical region.  A regular 317-point disk lattice is mapped into that
ellipse and triangulated, yielding a shape-regular disk mesh.  We call this a
\emph{digit-3-centered} region: the nearest posterior class and every decoded
image are displayed, rather than assuming that every point remains in one
class.

For a latent point $z$, let $D_\theta(z)$ be the decoded mean image.  We clip
it to $[0,1]$, average each $2\times2$ pixel block, and normalize the resulting
$14\times14$ array to unit mass.  Before normalization, we add $10^{-10}$ to
each pooled bin solely to avoid exact zero masses.  If $y_a\in[0,1]^2$ is the location of the
$a$th pooled pixel, the declared decoded-image measure is
\begin{equation}
 K_z=\sum_{a=1}^{196}p_a(z)\,\delta_{y_a}.
 \label{eq:mnist-image-measure}
\end{equation}
Every mesh-edge observation is the unregularized discrete Kantorovich value
\begin{equation}
 W_2(K_i,K_j)^2
 =\min_{\pi\mathbf1=p_i,\,\pi^{\trans}\mathbf1=p_j}
 \sum_{a,b}\pi_{ab}\norm{y_a-y_b}^2,
 \label{eq:mnist-discrete-ot}
\end{equation}
computed by the linear-program solver in POT \cite{flamary2021}.
It is not a pixelwise $\ell_2$ distance and has no entropic regularization
bias.  It is also not claimed to equal the VAE likelihood distance:
Equation~\eqref{eq:mnist-image-measure} is the declared spatial measure of
decoded ink.  Normalization deliberately removes total ink mass; unbalanced
OT would be needed to retain it.

The fitted set $Q$ consists of the 924 Delaunay edges.  We separately compute
direct $W_2$ on 2,036 nonedge pairs two graph hops apart.  Their displayed
Euclidean chord error is a held-out empirical diagnostic, not a quantity
covered by the PL intrinsic-distance certificate.  This is a transductive
pair holdout on the same 317 vertices, not generalization to unseen images or
latent points.

\paragraph{The controlled surface is recovered.}
The maximum discrepancy between the analytic Gaussian $W_2$ formula and the
hidden 3D chord is at machine precision.  The scaled latent plane retains
$7.35\%$ query RMS, whereas the selected best-of-three height-field branch
reaches $0.0007\%$ and
recovers the mixed-sign curvature to numerical precision.  Its aligned
coordinate error is also numerical.  This verifies that the pipeline does not
require constant curvature: the positive peaks, negative transition regions,
and saddle are all represented by one definition.

\paragraph{MNIST exposes a fidelity--readability trade-off.}
The scaled latent plane has $23.20\%$ query RMS.  Allowing every planar vertex
to move lowers this to $10.55\%$, but the minimum display angle falls to
$0.44^\circ$, the global $s_-$ to $0.011$, and some faces reverse orientation.
The selected smooth 3D branch attains nearly the same query RMS, $10.58\%$,
while retaining $[s_-,s_+]=[0.440,1.729]$ and an adjacent-normal fifth
percentile of $0.738$;
only $1.0\%$ of adjacent normal pairs are negative.  Its curvature-mass RMSE,
$0.733$, is only modestly below the scaled plane's $0.772$, so we do not claim
curvature recovery on MNIST.  Its maximum edge error is $71.7\%$ and
$\delta/\ell_{\min}=3.383$; the result is a comparably low \emph{average}
stress with substantially better nondegeneracy, not a uniformly low-error
map.  On held-out two-hop nonedges, direct-$W_2$ chord RMS is $40.9\%$ for the
scaled plane, $44.7\%$ for free 2D, $39.8\%$ for smooth 3D, and $27.5\%$ for
free 3D.  These large errors confirm that the edge audit does not control
direct nonedge Wasserstein chords.  Finally, the free 3D reference reaches
$0.039\%$ query RMS but has negative adjacent normals on $27.7\%$ of pairs.
Thus, under our declared regularization, the smooth 3D branch supplies a
noncollapsed display at essentially the same average stress as the degenerate
free-2D fit.  Local distance fit alone does not select a readable external
shape.  The smooth branch's nonzero edge residual is the $\delta$ audited by
Proposition~\ref{prop:pl-certificate}; it is not replaced by an unobservable
coordinate error.

\paragraph{What is certified.}
For each returned surface we compute $(s_-,s_+)$ and $\Delta_i$ directly from
the observed and displayed triangles, so
Equations~\eqref{eq:distance-certificate}--\eqref{eq:curvature-certificate}
bound every intrinsic distance and every curvature-mass change of the observed
PL metric.  We do not claim a validated floating-point $\varepsilon$ for the
MNIST OT solves, so that audit is relative to the computed unregularized-OT
edge lengths; a validated $\varepsilon$ would extend it to ideal edge-$W_2$
values through Equations~\eqref{eq:ideal-curvature-certificate}--
\eqref{eq:ideal-distance-certificate}.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The scope is intentionally two-dimensional.  A two-dimensional PL metric has
scalar angle-defect curvature and admits a natural surface-visualization
attempt in $\mathbb R^3$, although an exact embedded realization need not
exist.  An intrinsically three-dimensional manifold instead has a curvature
tensor and cannot in general be visualized without loss inside $\mathbb R^3$;
slices or a higher ambient dimension would be required.

Topology and the triangular complex are supplied, and the optimizer is not
proved to find a global stress minimum: the near-exact controlled recovery is
the best of three deterministic initializations, not evidence of optimizer
stability.  The controlled problem uses a designed Gaussian identity, while
MNIST uses one VAE seed, one digit-centered region, and pooled normalized
image mass; pooling changes the measure and normalization discards total ink.
Because our free 2D and smooth 3D fits use different display constraints,
their comparison does not by itself identify a pure dimension effect.
Finally, raw pointwise curvature density is unstable under fine-scale noise
according to Equation~\eqref{eq:density-scaling}; smoothing it with a
statistical guarantee remains separate work.

Within this scope, the paper's claim is simple and testable.  The ordinary
latent plot is replaced by a surface fitted to how decoded measures actually
move under optimal transport.  Its facewise distortion factors and
incident-angle changes directly bound intrinsic PL distance and
curvature-mass distortion.  That makes the 3D picture an auditable geometric
summary rather than an unexplained rendering.
