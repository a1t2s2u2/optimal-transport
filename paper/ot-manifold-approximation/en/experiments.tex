\section{A Visible Inverse Problem}
\label{sec:experiments}

The theory distinguishes two reconstruction questions.  Exact mesh-edge
lengths identify a same-skeleton convex polyhedron under
Definition~\ref{def:same-skeleton-model}; this is the general finite inverse
problem.  The experiment asks a narrower question whose answer can be seen:
if the hidden smooth surface is known to be a round sphere, can sparse local
Wasserstein observations unfold a collapsed planar representation back into
that sphere?  The round-sphere assumption is part of the estimator, not a
conclusion inferred from arbitrary data.

\subsection{A constant-curvature reconstruction algorithm}
\label{sec:spherical-estimator}

The estimator receives a triangular complex $\mathcal T$ and measured
Wasserstein chords $\widehat c_{ij}$ for a sparse local query set
$E_{\mathrm q}\supseteq E$.  It receives neither the hidden 3D vertices nor the
Gaussian parameters used to create the benchmark.

\paragraph{Curvature and scale.}
The subset $\{\widehat c_e:e\in E\}$ makes every face a Euclidean triangle.
Equations~\eqref{eq:angle-defect}--\eqref{eq:discrete-density} then give raw
angle-defect curvature and total PL area $\widehat A$.  Sphere topology gives
$\sum_i\widehat\kappa_i=4\pi$ exactly.  Under the additional constant-curvature
model $K=1/R^2$, Gauss--Bonnet therefore gives the scale estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat R=\sqrt{\frac{\widehat A}{4\pi}}.
 \label{eq:learned-radius}
\end{equation}
With exact lengths at finite resolution, this estimates the radius of the
inscribed PL surface and is slightly smaller than the smooth radius; noisy
lengths do not preserve that one-sided statement.

\paragraph{Local chords to global travel times.}
For every queried pair, we apply the spherical chord--arc conversion
\begin{equation}
 \widehat a_{ij}
 =2\widehat R\arcsin\!\left(
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{\widehat c_{ij}}{2\widehat R}\right\}\right).
 \label{eq:experimental-chord-arc}
\end{equation}
Shortest paths in the sparse graph $(V,E_{\mathrm q},\widehat a)$ produce an
all-pairs matrix $\widetilde D$.  The benchmark sampling includes antipodal
pairs, so we make one explicit diameter calibration,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat D
 =\frac{\pi\widehat R}{\max_{i,j}\widetilde D_{ij}}\widetilde D.
 \label{eq:diameter-calibration}
\end{equation}
This normalizes the graph diameter; direction-dependent path distortion
remains and is measured in the reported geodesic error.  The calibration is a
strong round-sphere and coverage assumption; an application without known
antipodal coverage may not use this step.

\paragraph{The reconstructed surface.}
A radius-$R$ sphere has Gram matrix
$G_{ij}=R^2\cos(d_{ij}/R)$.  We form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G_{ij}
 =\widehat R^2\cos(\widehat D_{ij}/\widehat R),
 \label{eq:spherical-gram}
\end{equation}
retain its three leading positive eigendirections, and project each resulting
row to radius $\widehat R$.  This is spherical classical scaling.  It makes
the experimental output easy to interpret, but it always returns a sphere.
It is therefore an illustration of constant-curvature metric recovery, not a
numerical solver for the general convex realization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex-identification}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:spherical-spectral-stability}
shows that this final spectral step is locally stable once $\widehat D$ and
$\widehat R$ are close; its distance-error term also keeps the preceding
graph and diameter approximations visible.

The full map is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &(\mathcal T,\text{local }W_2\text{ chords})\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow
 (\widehat\kappa,\widehat A,\widehat R)
 \longrightarrow\widehat D
 \longrightarrow\widehat X\subset\mathbb R^3 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:experimental-pipeline}
\end{equation}
Every operation before evaluation is a function of the stated input.

\subsection{Controlled data and negative controls}
\label{sec:controlled-data}

\paragraph{A disk that hides a sphere.}
We instantiate Proposition~\ref{prop:hidden-sphere} on the unit sphere with
$k=1/2$.  The Gaussian means $(x_1,x_2)$ occupy a doubly covered disk, whereas
the full-rank covariance path retains $x_3$ and is noncommuting between
different heights.  The exact identity
\begin{equation}
 W_2(\mu_x,\mu_y)=\norm{x-y}
 \label{eq:experiment-w2-chord}
\end{equation}
makes each observation a 3D chord, while the estimator receives only scalar
pairwise distances.  This
analytic decoder is intentional: it isolates the inverse-geometry error from
neural optimization and Monte Carlo OT error.

Uniform icosphere vertices would leak much of the answer through connectivity.
We therefore move them by the fixed spherical diffeomorphism
\begin{align}
 \phi'&=\phi+0.175\sin(2\phi),\notag\\
 \lambda'&=\lambda+0.24\sin^2(\phi'),
 \label{eq:sampling-warp}
\end{align}
which preserves the round surface and antipodal pairing but creates visibly
nonuniform vertex locations.  We use subdivision levels $0$ through $3$,
giving $12,42,162,$ and $642$ vertices.

The local query graph contains every pair within three mesh hops.  At the
finest level this is $11{,}370$ of $205{,}761$ possible pairs, or $5.53\%$.
Because mesh degree and hop radius are fixed, query count is $O(|V|)$ under
refinement.  The same noisy measurement is reused when a queried pair is a
triangle edge; curvature never receives an independent oracle edge.

We multiply every exact chord by
\begin{equation}
 \exp(\sigma Z-\sigma^2/2),
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
 \label{eq:multiplicative-noise}
\end{equation}
for $\sigma\in\{0,0.005,0.01,0.02\}$.  The noiseless condition is run once;
each noisy condition uses three deterministic seeds.  The exponential makes
lengths positive and mean preserving.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
All 3D errors use an orthogonal Procrustes alignment with translation but no
rescaling.  Ground-truth coordinates enter only here.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Topology only} aligns the original uniform icosphere to the warped
 target.  It has exactly the right connectivity and sphere prior but receives
 no measured length, directly testing topology leakage.
 \item \emph{Euclidean MDS} applies ordinary three-dimensional classical MDS
 to the same recovered global-distance matrix $\widehat D$.
 \item \emph{Mean only} embeds the Gaussian means, the collapsed disk visible
 to a deterministic decoder.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report relative Frobenius error of all-pairs great-circle distances,
corresponding-vertex RMSE after alignment, symmetric Hausdorff distance, and
area-weighted curvature RMSE.  Here aligned vertex RMSE is
$|V|^{-1/2}\min_{Q,b}\norm{\widehat X-(XQ+\mathbf 1b^\trans)}_F$.
For a queried pair set $\mathcal Q$, relative chord stress means
$\norm{(\widehat c^{\mathrm{rec}}-\widehat c^{\mathrm{obs}})_{\mathcal Q}}_2/
\norm{\widehat c^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathcal Q}}_2$.  The raw-parameter audit uses
$d_\alpha(i,j)^2=\norm{m_i-m_j}^2+\alpha^2\norm{C_i-C_j}_F^2$, where the
single $\alpha\ge0$ is chosen with ground truth to minimize relative distance
RMSE; it is an oracle baseline, not an estimator input.
Curvature is compared both with the noiseless angle-defect value on the same
mesh and with smooth $K=1$.  We also report the fraction of nonpositive angle
defects, because positivity is a theorem condition rather than a cosmetic
diagnostic.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:surface-results}

\paragraph{OT contains the missing coordinate.}
Across every trial, the maximum discrepancy between the general Gaussian
$W_2$ formula and the hidden 3D chord is at most $1.5\times10^{-15}$.  On the finest
query graph, the minimum covariance eigenvalue is $1.754$ and $98.35\%$ of
queried covariance pairs do not commute.  Even an oracle choice of one global
scale on raw covariance Frobenius differences leaves relative parameter-
distance RMSE $0.01245$, whereas the Wasserstein identity is at machine
precision.  Mean-only RMSE is $0.6538$, so the flat disk cannot represent the
target.  Figure~\ref{fig:wasserstein-surface-pipeline} shows that local
Wasserstein lengths restore the missing dimension.

\paragraph{Recovery improves with resolution.}
With exact observations, relative geodesic error decreases as
\begin{equation}
 0.0985,\ 0.0392,\ 0.00915,\ 0.00261
 \label{eq:observed-distance-sequence}
\end{equation}
over $12,42,162,642$ vertices.  Aligned 3D RMSE similarly decreases as
\begin{equation}
 0.1365,\ 0.0400,\ 0.0105,\ 0.00287.
 \label{eq:observed-shape-sequence}
\end{equation}
At the coarsest 12-vertex mesh, the PL radius underestimate makes the
chord--arc argument clip for $24.2\%$ of noiseless queries.  No noiseless
clipping occurs at levels 1--3, so it does not affect the three finer points or
the finest-mesh conclusions.
At 642 vertices the topology-only control remains at $0.1363$, ordinary MDS
at $0.3616$, and the mean disk at $0.6538$.  Thus connectivity plus an
$S^2$ output constraint is not sufficient to explain the result.  The
reconstructed query chords have relative stress $0.00330$ in the noiseless
case, quantifying the remaining graph and spectral approximation rather than
hiding it.  Symmetric Hausdorff error is $0.00337$ with exact lengths and
$0.00678$ at 1\% noise.

raises geodesic error only to $0.00437$ and 3D RMSE to $0.00349$ under the

\paragraph{Pointwise curvature is the hard quantity.}
With exact lengths, curvature relative to the noiseless PL target is zero to
machine precision; the remaining smooth-$K=1$ RMSE is $0.0218$ at 642
vertices and is a discretization effect.  Fixed relative noise behaves
differently.  At the same resolution, $0.5\%,1\%,$ and $2\%$ length noise
produce raw discrete-curvature RMSE $1.17,2.32,$ and $4.73$, respectively.
At 1\% noise, $32.6\%$ of vertices have nonpositive defect.

This is qualitatively consistent with the deterministic distinction in
Equation~\eqref{eq:noise-amplification}.  That theorem uses a maximum edge
error, whereas \eqref{eq:multiplicative-noise} draws independent log-normal
errors; for small $\sigma$, their maximum relative magnitude over a finite
query set is typically of order
$\sigma\sqrt{\log|E_{\mathrm q}|}$.  The sweep therefore tests the predicted
amplification pattern, not the theorem's constants.  If
$\varepsilon\asymp\eta h$, defect error is $O(\eta)$ but division by vertex
area makes curvature-density error $O(\eta/h^2)$.  Refinement at fixed
relative noise therefore helps global distance reconstruction while hurting
an unsmoothed pointwise derivative.
Consistent fine-scale curvature needs shrinking noise or a regularized
estimator.  In particular, the positive-defect condition
\eqref{eq:convexity-noise} does not hold in the noisy finest-mesh trials; their
good spherical outputs come from the declared constant-curvature projection,
not from an application of the convex-realization theorem.

\subsection{The same-skeleton step beyond a round sphere}
\label{sec:convex-realization-experiment}

The spherical sweep is visually simple but does not execute the general
realization step in Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}.  We therefore add a
small, separate distance-geometry experiment.  Its estimator receives only
$(|V|,F,E,\{\widehat\ell_e\}_{e\in E})$.  Spherical MDS supplies an initial
convex branch, after which continuation minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{(i,j)\in E}
 \left(
 \frac{\norm{X_i-X_j}-\widehat\ell_{ij}}
 {\overline\ell}
 \right)^2 .
 \label{eq:edge-realization-objective}
\end{equation}
Here $\overline\ell$ is the mean observed edge length.
One nondegenerate anchor triangle is placed from its three observed side
lengths, removing the Euclidean-motion gauge.  A local convexity penalty is
used only during 41 continuation stages.  The final least-squares polish has
117 free coordinates and 117 nonanchor edge residuals; it contains no radius,
row normalization, spherical loss, or ground-truth coordinate.

We test convex hulls of 42 samples from a sphere and from its linear image with
axes $(1.20,1.00,0.80)$.  The latter is an ellipsoidal convex polyhedron
sampling a smooth strictly convex surface of nonconstant Gaussian curvature.
The hidden-Gaussian construction still gives
$W_2(\mu_x,\mu_y)=\norm{x-y}$.  With exact chords, aligned RMSE is
$3.25\times10^{-15}$ on the sphere and $2.17\times10^{-15}$ on the ellipsoid,
although the ellipsoid's spherical initializer has RMSE $0.1270$.  At 0.5\%
edge noise, mean RMSE over three seeds is $0.00601$ and $0.00720$; at 1\%, it
is $0.01094$ and $0.01463$.  All 14 outputs retain all 42 convex-hull vertices
and exactly the prescribed 80 facets.

Exact fitting of the noisy cases is not denoising.  A triangulated sphere has
$|E|=3|V|-6$, and local rigidity maps a sufficiently small perturbed length
vector to a nearby, generally different convex polyhedron.  The observed
coordinate error is the stability quantity in Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}.
The reported RMSE is its $|V|^{-1/2}$ normalization.  The true rigidity
matrices have full rank $120=3|V|-6$, with $s_X=0.352$ on the sphere and
$0.287$ on the ellipsoid.  All 12 noisy trials satisfy the local scale
$2\norm{\widehat\ell-\ell}_2/s_X$; the largest observed error-to-bound ratio
is $0.233$.  This prototype verifies the intended local branch numerically;
it does not prove global convergence of continuation.

\section{Scope and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

The controlled result establishes a precise, visually testable statement:
for a probabilistic decoder whose local Wasserstein chords encode a hidden
round metric, sparse queries can recover global travel times and the spherical
surface without decoder derivatives.  The theorem separately states what
edge lengths determine for a general same-skeleton strictly convex
polyhedron, and gives an end-to-end sphere error decomposition in
\eqref{eq:end-to-end-sphere}.  That decomposition concerns a nearby
same-skeleton convex realization; it is not a guarantee for the implemented
graph-shortest-path and spherical-scaling pipeline.

Several assumptions remain visible.  Topology and triangulation are supplied.
The spherical sweep assumes constant curvature, known antipodal coverage, and
a Gaussian benchmark with exact $W_2$; its all-pairs numerical stage uses
$O(|V|^2)$ memory.  The edge-only experiment has only 42 vertices and three
noisy seeds per condition, requires continuation to remain on the correct
convex branch, and does not establish global convergence.  Neither experiment
infers a true geometry for MNIST,
smooths curvature, or gives a minimax statistical rate.  A learned decoder
would additionally introduce finite-sample and numerical OT errors; these
enter the edge-error term of the theory but are not evaluated here.

The main conceptual change from Jacobian-based latent geometry is therefore
simple: geometry can be treated as an inverse problem from observable local
distribution distances.  In the controlled case, the answer is not merely a
curvature heat map.  It is a reconstructed surface whose error can be split
into Wasserstein observation error and finite-mesh approximation error.
